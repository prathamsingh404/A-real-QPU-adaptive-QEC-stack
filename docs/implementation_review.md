# Research and implementation review, 19 November 2026

## What was searched

I searched for adaptive matching weights, syndrome-only noise estimation, drift detection and recalibration, quantum decoder priors, hardware memory data, correlated matching, neural predecoding and real-time decoding. Sources included arXiv, APS, Nature, OpenAlex, institutional publications, authors' code, model cards, PyPI and Zenodo. Direct Google Scholar requests returned HTTP 429 and an automated-traffic challenge. OpenAlex independently surfaced Bhardwaj, DGR and differentiable maximum-likelihood estimation. Dates below distinguish preprints and updates from peer-reviewed work. Coverage is targeted to surface-code decoding and the closest related hardware or software. It cannot certify global priority.

## Closest work on the algorithm

| Work | What is already known | What this repository actually tests |
|---|---|---|
| Spitz et al. | Estimate matching weights from observed correlations as physical noise changes. | A Spitz-style graph estimator under six update schedules and a fixed factory reference. The estimator and label-free operation have prior art. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02360) |
| Wang et al., DGR | Track decoded edge occurrences, then also use edge co-occurrences for conditional matching. | We implemented the occurrence-frequency stage, used finite log odds and scored the following window on public shots. No result for their full correlation stage. [Methods, IV-A and IV-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16214v3) |
| Bhardwaj et al., PRX Quantum 2026 | Sliding, overlapping and spectral approaches to estimating time-varying noise. | Two-window pooling is a simpler control. Their full tracker remains a relevant direct algorithmic comparison. [Paper](https://journals.aps.org/prxquantum/abstract/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5) · [Authors' repository](https://github.com/Dev-ph-quan-1203/Estimation-of-Drifting-Noise-in-QEC) |
| Sivak, Newman and Klimov | Optimize decoder priors with a logical-performance objective. | Scored published predictions using optimized priors separately. The input information and training regime differ from online syndrome-only updates. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02700) |
| Differentiable maximum likelihood | Fits physical noise parameters and evaluates them on experimental Google data. | Another reason an accuracy claim needs stronger learned priors. Their training objective and inference workload differ from a fixed number of matching-graph fits. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19722) · [Released code](https://github.com/CHY-i/DMLE-QEC) |
| ReloQate, 2026 preprint | Predicts logical performance from detector rates and remaps patches during recalibration. | Different physical intervention. It directly precedes the idea of a detector alarm triggering action. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00837) |
| Local clustering decoder | Updates a decoding graph on the fly in response to control signals and implements an adaptive hardware decoder. | Our detector-alarm study makes no FPGA or first-adaptive-graph claim. [Published paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66773-x) |
| Hoyt et al., September 2026 preprint | Finds model mismatch across consecutive IBM acquisition windows and investigates independent Google data. | Physical chronology and model-compatibility signals are directly relevant. Our original 105-qubit replay lacks verified chronology. The separate 72-qubit set 1 extension uses eight documented acquisition samples per basis. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29018) |
| QAdapt, arXiv v3, 18 November 2026 | Continual adaptation in a neural predecoder, compound-noise transfer and native Willow evaluation. | A close precedent for a broad adaptive-decoder claim. The endpoint is equivalent per-round risk and the comparison is between trained pipelines. We have no head-to-head result against QAdapt. [Latest version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28422v3) |

## NVIDIA, IBM and Google

**NVIDIA Ising.** The surface-code work combines a learned local predecoder with a global backend. Its noise-learning component also infers weights from syndromes. The public repository supplies training and inference code; Fast and Accurate have separately published model cards. I inspected the upstream README, its offline sample requirements, the parameterized noise model and tests at revision 26305d7e. The offline pipeline reconstructs a circuit and checks the basis, orientation, detector count and noise metadata. Reordering or relabeling Google's XZZX records without validating detector parity and the logical map could produce a plausible-looking, meaningless score. I exercised NVIDIA's pinned sparse sample writer on both 44,000-row records. Every bit survived a Stim readback (104 detectors and one observable per row). This validates data transport. We have no validated geometry adapter or end-to-end Ising score for these two records. NVIDIA's GB300 per-round times use different hardware and units from our host microseconds per 2,000 completed memory shots. Its color-code release concerns a further code family. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12841) · [Pinned implementation](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Ising-Decoding/tree/26305d7e90428761bbe552caaa8676b9bdc44b3c) · [Fast model](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Ising-Decoder-SurfaceCode-1-Fast) · [Accurate model](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Ising-Decoder-SurfaceCode-1-Accurate) · [Color-code release](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-ising-decoding-cuts-color-code-logical-error-rates-by-over-300x/)

**IBM and AMD.** Relay-BP is a belief-propagation decoder with a memory schedule. IBM-affiliated authors evaluate circuit noise including bivariate bicycle and surface codes. The authors release a Rust implementation with Python bindings. Pêlagos extends this line to GPU simulation and multi-GPU orchestration; its November 12 preprint is relevant to throughput. Neither optimizes the timing of one parity-estimator fit. I checked the release assets and PyPI for Relay-BP 0.2.2. The available binary was CPython 3.14 Linux, with no Windows wheel, and this workstation's WSL distribution could not mount its disk. There is no measured Relay-BP result in this study. Separately, a PNNL public dataset supplies IBM repetition-memory records and calibration snapshots across three processors. I checksum-verified its Fez archive, which contains 41 snapshots, and scored all distance-three, one-round snapshots using terminal majority. Across ten parallel chains per shot, known preparations X0, X1, Z0 and Z1 gave 49, 179, 9 and 100 wrong majorities per 20,480 chain outcomes respectively. Each preparation contributed 2,048 device shots. This is an external repetition-memory format and scoring check. It cannot establish surface-code adaptivity or restore the missing Marrakesh jobs. The retained results and executable are in data/external_ibm_majority.json and scripts/inspect_public_ibm.py. [IBM decoder paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01779) · [Author implementation](https://github.com/trmue/relay) · [Release files](https://pypi.org/project/relay-bp/0.2.2/#files) · [Pêlagos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16285) · [Experimental data](https://zenodo.org/records/20768087)

**Google and DeepMind.** AlphaQubit 2 studies accelerator-based, high-throughput neural decoding. Google's Willow work goes further than offline tracking: reinforcement-learning control used detection events to steer physical parameters during QEC. A preprint posted November 16 studies temporally correlated noise and adaptive termination of syndrome extraction. These are three distinct interventions. Their results rule out claims that this project introduced adaptive QEC, proved real-time operation or is the first to respond to syndrome evidence. They do not decide whether a one-fit software policy beats a calendar on identical trajectories. [AlphaQubit 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07737) · [Physical closed-loop control](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10759-2) · [November preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578)

**Same-shot comparison actually performed.** I ran local PyMatching with and without correlations and scored four Google prediction streams from the original release. Every column uses the same 44,000 observed labels per basis. Local correlated matching gave 4,205 X and 3,607 Z errors. Google's fixed-SI1000 correlated predictions gave 4,086 and 3,419. Its fixed-SI1000 Harmony ensemble gave 3,889 and 3,089. The selective policy gave 4,680 and 4,039. The optimized-prior versions gave 3,643/2,960 for correlated matching and 3,517/2,899 for Harmony. The publisher trained those priors on 13-cycle calibration data; independence from these particular rows is unverified. The fixed-prior comparisons alone establish the shortfall. [Source and upstream format](https://zenodo.org/records/13273331) · [Local counts](../data/public_decoder_comparison.json)

## Publication implication

The defensible difference is the controlled timing experiment: use exactly one fit, keep the updated graph for the same length of time, train the calendar on development seeds, then compare on fresh paired trajectories. We found a small advantage under one family of transient hotspots. The literature search gives no basis to declare a first-in-field algorithm, a new error-correction code, or performance leadership. A methods paper should explain why the timing question matters after accounting for monitoring overhead and why a reader should expect it to transfer to an experimental stream.


## Correlated matching and experimental comparators

Darmawan quantified how much performance is lost under deliberate mischaracterization of local noise using near-optimal tensor-network decoding. Our hindsight calculation addresses a narrower question on finite trajectories: how many errors the available update actions could save if a selector knew their future labels. Schwartzman-Nowik et al. go further than the empirical arm tested here by discovering missing correlated mechanisms and assigning their logical effects. Their November 4 preprint reports gains with correlated matching on physical Google data at distances 5–7. This materially weakens any novelty claim based on preserving correlations or adapting decoder priors. [Darmawan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08706) · [Schwartzman-Nowik et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06605)

The QAdapt revision of November 18 evaluates a continually trained neural predecoder and transfers its frozen weights to Willow records. Its equivalent per-round rates, reference models and training resources differ from our absolute per-memory-shot error counts. Google’s November 16 stability study identifies long-lived leakage and tests adaptive termination of syndrome extraction offline. It varies the measurement duration within a memory experiment. Our policies keep that duration fixed and change the decoder across independent shots. [QAdapt v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28422v3) · [Google stability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578)

NVIDIA’s November 16 decoding server specifies a streaming interface and evaluates backend throughput and latency, including queueing considerations. IBM and AMD’s November 12 Pêlagos study distinguishes GPU-only and end-to-end throughput while scaling Relay-BP across multiple GPUs on gross codes. Both give concrete examples of what an engineering claim about real-time performance requires. Our timers cover CPU decode and policy work on batches of independent shots. We did not run these accelerator pipelines. [NVIDIA decoding server](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/11/16/cudaq-qec-decoding-server/) · [Pêlagos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16285)

On a separate Willow release we scored all four supplied decoder prediction streams on exactly the same 384,000 labels as our seven arms. The frozen hotspot library selected the factory graph throughout. Released sparse blossom, both Tesseract pathways and AlphaQubit 2 each made fewer errors in every record. Using published predictions permits a direct check of accuracy. It gives no local inference timings or independent evidence of train/test separation for the publisher’s optimized models. The number of released records with acquisition chronology depends on the dataset: the four Willow records here document patch, basis and round count, whereas Hoyt et al. describe ordered acquisition samples in the separate 72-qubit set 1 release.
