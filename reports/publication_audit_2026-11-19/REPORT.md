# Publication audit of the adaptive QEC project

**19 November 2026 · Scientific and software review for the authors · main at 77861b1f79807b5d82806e90b73c53b82d61002c**

**Editorial decision — hold submission and continue the research.** The project contains a working surface-code simulator and decoding experiment, a recoverable numerical result from an earlier controller, genuine software engineering, and hardware-facing experiments with recorded IBM job identifiers. The present manuscript claims a deployable advantage, statistical guarantees, and measured latency beyond what those artifacts establish. I would recommend rejection and invite a substantially redesigned experiment. I would encourage a student to pursue that experiment.

The most valuable finding is a failure mode with a usable explanation. Under one fixed local noise mismatch, an ordinary decoder that keeps learning from the previous batch beats the proposed conservative gate at distance three. On very short windows the same ordinary update is destructive. The choice of *when to trust a new calibration* is worth studying. The open problem is whether the gate can make that choice more effectively than simple, fairly tuned alternatives at equal latency and update cost.

## Audit scope and method

I inventoried the 250 tracked files, structurally parsed all 168 tracked Python files, and traced the confirmatory suite from YAML and circuit construction through decoder selection, scoring, per-trajectory JSON, statistics, figures, tables, and manuscript prose. I examined the physical-job producers and their stored results, the hardware replay, generic runtime, calibrator, competing controller paths, benchmark and CI checks, earlier experiment outputs, Git history, and the publication lock. The deepest review concerns code that actually produces evidence for the paper. Peripheral application, UI, and teaching code received a structural and claims review. This is not a line-by-line certification of every peripheral method.

I used an isolated checkout for execution. The full current suite used 30 seeds, two distances, four conditions, three window sizes and 10,000 shots per run. The 720 rows reuse seeds and syndrome streams across window sizes. They represent 720 configurations and seed combinations, not 720 independent replications of H1. All four arms in an individual run see identical sampled shots. The test environment used Python 3.11.16, Stim 1.16.0, PyMatching 2.4.0, NumPy 2.4.6 and SciPy 1.17.1. The original manifest lists Python 3.14.2 and the same Stim and PyMatching versions. I separately tested the historical implementation, equal-information timing, the circuit fault targets, and a regularized comparator. Literature and public code were reviewed through 19 November 2026. No IBM account, provider job retrieval, new QPU execution, or independent hardware dataset replay was used.

## What the four headline numbers actually measure

### 1. The localized-hotspot gain belongs to an older, same-batch experiment

Each row below uses the same 30 seeds and 300,000 shots at distance three and window size 2,000. LER is the fraction of complete shots with a logical error. “Next batch” means the graph available before the first shot of the scored batch. The p-values are two-sided, unadjusted paired t-tests across seeds versus factory matching. These rows use shared observations and should not be counted as independent experiments.

| Decoder and timing | Errors | LER | Change vs static | Raw p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Static factory matching | 7,660 | 2.5533% | — | — |
| Archived gate, estimates from scored batch | 7,531 | 2.5103% | −129 errors | 0.01453 |
| Historical gate, next batch | 7,624 | 2.5413% | −36 errors | 0.30908 |
| Current gate, estimates from scored batch | 7,625 | 2.5417% | −35 errors | 0.16936 |
| Current gate, next batch | 7,662 | 2.5540% | +2 errors | 0.92176 |
| Ungated Spitz-style update, next batch | 6,446 | 2.1487% | −1,214 errors | 2.02 × 10⁻¹⁴ |
| Matched-noise MWPM reference | 5,354 | 1.7847% | −2,306 errors | — |

The archived comparison gives a −0.000430 absolute LER change, or −0.043 percentage points and approximately −1.68% relative. Its ordinary 95% paired interval is [−0.0007683, −0.0000917]. Eight trajectories improved, 22 tied, and none worsened. An exact sign test on the eight nonzero differences gives p = 0.0078125. There is an empirical signal in the old comparison. It is smaller than the protocol's stated minimum practically relevant change of 0.0006. The roughly 30% best-case reference improvement is the benefit of giving MWPM the simulated noise model. The gate captures about 5.6% of that available error-count difference in the archived test and essentially none in the current forward test.

The reported gate may estimate a graph from the entire scored batch before decoding it. The ungated arm always learns from the preceding batch. This is a valid distinction between two service models. A system allowed to buffer and retrospectively decode a whole batch can legitimately use that batch's *unlabeled* syndromes. A claim of online superiority requires the same buffering permission and delay for every competitor. The suite passes logical labels to its scoring code, and the gate itself receives syndromes only. The flaw is unequal access to time-indexed observations, not evidence of logical labels entering this gate. [Experiment loop](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/experiments/run_suite.py#L169-L197)

Of the 720 complete records, 592 matched exactly in the fresh current-code rerun. All 128 disagreements were in the CG-CCADS arm. Static, ungated and matched-noise reference records agreed throughout. The earlier implementation standardized against a scalar reference using the present window's standard error; the current gate uses a two-sample Welch statistic when it keeps the first window's samples. On the primary hotspot the older policy reweighted 13 of 150 windows and the current policy reweighted four. This explains why a small source change can erase the headline effect even when every control arm reproduces. [Current decision path](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/src/adaptive_qec/controller/reweighting_gate.py#L223-L263)

### 2. Small samples really can damage an unregularized update

At distance three, equally weighting the four labeled scenarios, the archived static LER is 0.0200933. At W = 200 unconditional raw reweighting raises it to 0.0410667, a 104.38% relative increase. On the same W the archived gate gives 0.0201517 and stays on 97.28% of decisions. The widely repeated 92.7% comes from W = 2,000, where the archived gate gives 0.0200442. Table 2 takes the full-gate row from the large window and places it alongside the small-window naive row. It does not isolate the effect of removing each gate. In particular, “net value” in that table is just static LER minus arm LER. It deducts no measured graph rebuild, waiting, or switching cost. [Ablation aggregation](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/src/adaptive_qec/evaluation/empirical_scaling_and_ablation.py#L557-L625)

There is also a price for abstaining. In the distance-three hotspot, ungated next-batch updating at W = 2,000 improves over static on **all 30 seeds**, with a relative reduction of 15.85%. A rule that rarely updates protects against noisy short-window estimates but can leave most of the benefit of a persistent local mismatch unused. A paper needs to quantify both costs.

### 3. Distance scaling is condition-specific

At W = 2,000 the archived stationary data give static LER 0.011547 → 0.007490 from d = 3 to 5, and gated LER 0.011603 → 0.007520. The corresponding ratios 1.542 and 1.543 are descriptive suppression under that condition. The stationary rates 0.00693 and 0.00449 in the claims ledger disagree with the published table. Under the uniform higher-noise setting the archived ratios instead are 0.947 static and 0.943 gated. The uniform setting at d = 5 gives 93 more gated errors in 300,000 shots than static (raw paired p = 0.00706). Under the d = 5 hotspot at W = 200 the archived gate also worsens LER (raw p = 0.00052). These are exploratory comparisons showing why a universal safety statement is untenable. At d = 5, W = 2,000 the archived hotspot advantage is only 13 errors, raw p = 0.0908. The ungated d = 3 win also fails to generalize automatically to d = 5.

The observations are per memory experiment with rounds = distance. The study changes both distance and shot duration. Two sizes at one nominal physical rate cannot determine a threshold, prove an exponential law, or establish an improved threshold from gating. A matched-noise graph is a sensible diagnostic reference. Finite samples, graph decomposition and the difference between the most likely individual correction and the most likely logical class preclude interpreting it as a universal LER bound. ### 4. The published latency benchmark does not execute the components named in the table

The table generator times fifty repetitions of two sleeps with requested durations of 30 and 10 microseconds. It supplies readout and dispatch as constants of 1.5 and 2.0 microseconds. The result labels 591.50 µs as a PyMatching solve and 317.51 µs as the controller decision, then reports 912.51 µs end to end. The operation measured is the host's sleep/wake timing. These figures must be withdrawn as decoder or controller measurements. The AST checker explicitly allows both sleep durations. Profiling utilities elsewhere in the tree cannot retrospectively supply the missing measurements. ## Does the physical experiment implement the stated model?

| Stated condition | Executed behavior | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Stationary baseline | Repeated draws of a fixed rotated Z-memory Stim circuit with four base noise settings of 0.004. | Valid simulation reference for that circuit. |
| Uniform ramp | A second fixed circuit has those settings at 0.007 from its very first shot. | Tests static calibration mismatch. The controller never observes a ramp between windows. |
| Local drift reaching 5% | YAML injects 4% single-qubit depolarization on the central data qubit after selected ticks, on top of the base model. The same circuit is sampled throughout. | Tests a persistent spatial mismatch with a specific within-shot fault schedule. There is no change point across monitoring windows. |
| Structural X/Z anisotropy | The inserted Z_ERROR instruction has no qubit targets. Stim accepts it. The resulting DEM equals the stationary DEM exactly at both distances. | A second effective stationary-noise model gets a distinct scenario label, inflating the apparent diversity of controls and its weight in pooled means. |

The hotspot target is genuinely a central data qubit at both distances. The string insertion checks for a literal line starting with TICK, missing the indented ticks inside Stim's REPEAT block. Inspection of the flattened circuits found only seven added 4% instructions at either distance, among 21 ticks at d = 3 and 35 at d = 5. The relative duration and placement of the injected disturbance therefore change with distance. Different sample counts between the anisotropy and stationary records do not repair identical underlying DEMs.

The runner draws 10,000 independent shots of the chosen circuit and partitions them into nonoverlapping batches. Those are not 10,000 consecutive QEC cycles of one logical memory. Its first window sets the scalar defect-rate reference from the already-mismatched population. Even when the factory graph is wrong, the monitored statistic can remain stationary. A statistically unusual first batch can influence all subsequent alarms, while a persistent mismatch can be large enough to reward ungated learning and almost invisible to the gate. The experiments with changing noise elsewhere in the repository do not feed this paper's primary table. [Circuit and sampling code](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/experiments/run_suite.py#L60-L164) · [Suite settings](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/configs/suite.yaml)

## Statistics, guarantees, and research records

1. **The confirmatory decision changes with the specified test.** The plan requires Holm adjustment across confirmatory regimes. Taking the four Table 1 rows as the family, the smallest raw p = 0.01452695 becomes 0.0581078, missing 0.05. A predeclared three-test family would give 0.04358; a single primary test would give 0.01453. The documents do not establish a prospectively frozen narrower family that justifies reclassifying the four-row result after inspection. The suite writes raw t-test p-values and ordinary t intervals. It does not implement the promised Holm/Wilcoxon pipeline. The historical sensitivity signal survives a sign test, but that cannot substitute for the stated primary test. [Analysis plan](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN.md#L98-L112) · [Aggregation code](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/experiments/run_suite.py#L300-L345)
2. **Safety needs its own endpoint.** Failure to reject zero difference does not establish equivalence or zero cost. Specify an acceptable absolute excess LER and show that a one-sided upper bound lies below it. Also report unnecessary updates, delay until a beneficial update, harmful updates, and reversion after recovery. The archived primary hotspot has 13 reweights, 130 stays and seven resets out of 150 decisions. Stationary has 149 stays and one reweight. Uniform has 129 stays, 14 reweights and seven resets. These give 86.7%, 99.3% and 86.0% stays respectively. Some ledger percentages differ appreciably. The table generator labels the primary hotspot STAY from an aggregate effect-size threshold despite actual reweights, while the ledger labels it ADAPT. These are three different quantities.
3. **Gate 1 in the published experiment has no CUSUM accumulation.** The benchmark imports ConfidenceGatedReweightingController. The separate CGCCADSController in another module has multi-timescale logic, including CUSUM. Its existence cannot support a description of the benchmarked decision rule. The plan lists thresholds of 3.0 and 0.005 while the runner hardcodes 2.5 and 0.002, with an adaptation tax of 0.003. The reweight barrier is tax plus dead zone. With a nonnegative tax, the dead-zone and tax tests on the same scalar reduce to one effective action threshold after Gate 1; changing the reason code does not change the graph. A component-level novelty claim needs an experiment that distinguishes the components. [Actual controller](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/src/adaptive_qec/controller/reweighting_gate.py#L269-L315) · [Other controller](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/src/adaptive_qec/controller/cg_ccads.py)
4. **The confidence guarantee lacks the needed uncertainty model.** Each edge probability is a nonlinear function of several sample means, covariances, clips, and for boundary edges a product over neighbors. The implemented standard error treats it as a directly observed binomial probability. Bonferroni can account for dependence *between tests* if the individual bounds are valid. It cannot give these plug-in bounds correct marginal coverage. Repeated looks across windows introduce a separate error-control question. An edge-shift bound is also a different object from a bound on logical-error improvement minus measured costs. The theory would need assumptions and validation linking those quantities. [Estimator](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/src/adaptive_qec/decoders/dem_calibration.py) · [Theoretical claims](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/docs/THEORETICAL_PROOFS_AND_BOUNDS.md)
5. **Uniform drift has a restricted invariance argument.** Adding a constant to each edge preserves the ordering of paths with equal edge counts. Competing correction paths can use different numbers of edges. For example, one edge of weight 3 loses to two of weight 1.4 each. Adding 1 makes the costs 4 and 4.8, reversing the winner. Furthermore the exact log-odds shift under probability rescaling depends on the original probability. The proof file includes an equal-cardinality qualification; the README extrapolates it to all relevant paths. Negligible average benefit in a particular simulated regime is a defensible empirical question. [Proof document](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/docs/THEORETICAL_PROOFS_AND_BOUNDS.md#L125-L150) · [README generalization](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/README.md#L1947-L1948)
6. **The historical design record does not demonstrate a prospective freeze of this exact test.** The manifest names 03090564. That Git object exists, but its tree lacks the runner, suite YAML, and scored controller. A nearby historical revision, c5a7a71, contains the inputs and results together. Its plan explicitly described an internal specification awaiting external deposit. Later revisions synchronized the registration text to observed results. The earlier PREREG at the manifest revision describes a broader study, and the committed power report lists localized hotspot as exploratory. The disclosed chronology supports a retrospective or internally specified analysis. It does not establish misconduct or exclude an independent dated record the authors might possess. [Manifest](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/data/manifest.json) · [Historical study revision](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/commit/c5a7a71) · [Power artifact](https://github.com/prathamsingh404/A-real-QPU-adaptive-QEC-stack/blob/77861b1/data/power_analysis_report.json)
7. **The power and provenance claims need numerical reconciliation.** With effect 0.0006/0.0011, 30 trajectories and a two-sided noncentral t-test, power is approximately 82.31% at α = 0.05, or 62.88% at the first four-test Holm threshold 0.0125. The stated 84.1% does not follow from those inputs; the power script instead sets its target standardized effect to 0.60. Planned power is conditional on the assumed effect and variance. The existing lock passes all 11 hashes on this Windows checkout. Seven text hashes differ on the actual LF Git blobs and only four PDFs match those blob hashes. That matters to the Linux CI workflow. The manifest's results hash matches its writer's JSON reserialization. The current config hash differs from the old manifest after a later comment edit; that alone does not imply a scientific change. ## Independent comparator experiment

I added one exploratory baseline for this audit. At the end of each batch it computes the repository's Spitz-style probabilities, averages each probability 50:50 with the corresponding factory probability, converts the result to a log-odds weight, and uses that graph on the **next** batch. The first batch always uses the factory graph. It preserves fault IDs and graph topology. I chose equal weighting as a simple default and did not search over mixing weights. This audit was designed after seeing the archived results and uses already-inspected seeds. It is a diagnostic of baseline strength; independent confirmation would require fresh trajectories. It also rebuilds every batch, so this comparison addresses LER, not the best trade-off at a fixed rebuild budget.

| Distance-three scenario | Static | 50:50 at W = 200 | 50:50 at W = 2,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stationary | 1.1547% | 1.1970% | 1.1573% |
| Uniform higher noise | 3.1977% | 3.3643% | 3.1860% |
| Local hotspot | 2.5533% | **1.9240%** | **1.9987%** |
| Nominal anisotropy, same DEM as stationary | 1.1317% | 1.1817% | 1.1363% |
| Equal-weight descriptive mean | 2.0093% | 1.9168% | 1.8696% |

At W = 200 the local hotspot is the decisive example. Unregularized reweighting gives 4.1570%, the archived gate gives 2.5737%, and the simple prior blend gives **1.9240%**. Small-window data can therefore be useful in precisely the condition where the draft argues for abstention. The raw estimator's performance penalty is real. Its size is an insufficient basis for saying that small-window *adaptation itself* is destructive. At W = 2,000, the blend makes 5,996 errors, saving 1,664 versus static. The current forward gate makes 7,662. The blend incurs modest penalties in some controls, particularly W = 200 uniform noise, so the next scientific question concerns safety margins and measured costs under different deployment conditions. The pooled mean is illustrative because the suite effectively counts a stationary DEM twice.

A second full rerun, this time from historical revision **c5a7a71**, reproduced **720/720** archived trajectory records. The historical gate still lost most of its primary gain when moved to next-window timing. Thus the original data have a recoverable computational origin, current main and the archived paper represent different policies, and a timing-matched test challenges the conclusion under *both* versions. This distinction matters for an honest correction.

## Hardware and the rest of the project

| Subsystem | Evidence and judgment |
|---|---|
| IBM repetition-code pilot | Records name ibm_marrakesh, two jobs, distance three, two rounds, 500 shots per arm, and report 19/500 MWPM discrepancies without DD versus 3/500 with DD. Scripts build circuits, submit via SamplerV2 and parse returned registers. That is credible evidence of hardware-facing engineering. The raw ordered QEC registers are absent from the published artifact, preventing an independent end-to-end reconstruction. |
| Validity of the reported hardware logical-error count | The parser scores a majority vote of the three final bits against a matcher whose Stim logical observable is the last data measurement, rec[-1]. They can differ. With clean earlier measurements and a single flip of the last readout bit, Stim's flag is 1, MWPM predicts 1, and the majority label is 0. The script counts this correct prediction for the modeled observable as a failure. Recompute both hardware arms using a declared observable, with a separate majority-only baseline. The magnitude and direction of the correction require the raw registers. |
| Dynamical decoupling | The two hardware arms are separate submissions. Their circuit builder inserts consecutive X,Y,X,Y gates inside barriers without specifying free-evolution intervals. A schedule and final transpiled pulses are needed to establish what executed, and interleaved repeated blocks to attribute the difference to DD. A bit-flip repetition memory also has limited sensitivity to pure phase noise. |
| Paired hardware replay | The replay reads hardware metadata and a defect rate, computes an unused defect count, and samples a fresh Stim repetition circuit at fixed 1% depolarizing probability with seed 101. Neither physical bitstrings nor the measured defect count drive its samples. The output sits in the hardware results directory with copied job IDs and no top-level simulation label. Its 1/500 factory result versus the stored hardware summary's 19/500 has a straightforward explanation. This particular output is synthetic and must be relabeled, moved, or regenerated before submission. |
| Other IBM pilots | The [[4,2,2]] records show 175/1,000 syndrome-flagged shots without DD and 488/1,000 with DD. Those are flag frequencies; without an injected error ground truth they do not measure detector sensitivity. The dynamic experiment logs 18 feedforward triggers in 1,000 shots and a parity metric, with no timing trace. The molecular IQPE artifact reports 761.5 kcal/mol error and chemical accuracy false. The teleportation example evaluates a known input state, with 0.919 reported dynamic fidelity and 0.979 for its SWAP comparison. A single known input does not certify a universal teleportation channel. These pilots establish scope and hands-on work. None of them exercised the paper's adaptive surface-code gate. |
| Generic runtime and dashboard | The installed qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.49.0 accepts SamplerV2(mode=...), while the generic live loop calls SamplerV2(session=...). Its parser treats the last bit of a single register as an observable and earlier bits as detectors, whereas the hardware runner uses several registers and parity differences. The generic dashboard gap endpoint explicitly says its prior experiment used Stim before assigning that LER to a hardware-named variable. These are material integration gaps for a claim of end-to-end physical operation. The dedicated submission scripts use a separate entry point. |
| Noise models, calibration, topology and digital twin | The repository includes per-qubit calibration data, simulated drift/bursts, a topology mapper and embedding code. Independently comparing the 133-qubit Torino graph with the installed IBM FakeTorino snapshot gave all 150 undirected couplers in agreement. That is a real implementation success. It is distinct from the 156-qubit Marrakesh target. The confirmatory suite never loads either device topology or the live calibration snapshot. A per-qubit DEM calibration route explicitly averages the qubit rates as a fallback. The surface-code table therefore describes an abstract lattice. |
| Decoder families, scheduler, mitigation and bandit | PyMatching, a custom union-find-style decoder, lazy fallback, X/Z scheduling, DD, burst/leakage and bandit controllers form a substantial prototype. The custom union-find implementation is described in the ledger as a heuristic, and the ledger retracts regret guarantees for a bandit with heuristic overrides. The full-adaptive simulator updates its controller with reward 1 − LER derived from known simulated observables. This can be useful for a labeled memory benchmark; it does not establish the gate's deployment boundary for a computation with unknown logical outcomes. Separate those systems by the information they consume. |
| Older stress tests | A tracked 50,000-shot test of an earlier adaptive scheme records static LER 0.17032 versus adaptive 0.21756, a 27.74% relative deterioration. A local, untracked d = 7 exploratory file for the earlier scheme also reports deterioration, and does not extend the present gate's confirmatory distances. These are different policies and noise models. Their results motivate reporting failures and cannot be pooled with the paper's four-arm study. |
| Testing, artifacts and examples | 513 tests passed with two warnings. A test checking that a hardware JSON exists validates file presence; a first-window STAY test exercises the branch that initializes its own reference; the whitelist tests detect certain float literals. None independently validates hardware origin, timing, family-wise coverage, or predictive success. Four methods in the scored gate class have later definitions that shadow earlier definitions, including reference reset and state restoration. The latter restores the reweighted flag without restoring the active graph. The data file also contains nonstandard NaN literals for degenerate comparisons. These are concrete fixes for a reproducible software release. |
| Public presentation | The hardware plotting script supplies literal comparison latencies including a 15,000 µs AlphaQubit point and a 12 µs “this work” point, assigning the DD arm's LER to the latter. These numbers do not form a controlled comparison between algorithms. The manuscript's journal-volume placeholder, membership and funding text should be checked by the authors before circulation. Earlier retractions in VALIDATION are a useful start; the synthetic replay and sleep-timed table are still live issues. |

Hardware evidence is grounded in the dedicated runner and stored summaries. The assessment of job provenance is limited to locally inspectable records. Nothing here establishes that the original IBM jobs themselves were fabricated. **Novelty judgment.** I found no basis for a priority claim covering adaptive MWPM, label-free graph calibration, the discovery of small-sample noise, or generic drift-triggered intervention. I also found no source in this review demonstrating that this exact choice of reference statistic, edge-shift threshold and abstention rule had already been published verbatim. An exact combination can be new and still have little scientific value. The demanding and credible contribution would be an independently validated decision rule or an experimentally grounded account of where regularization, always-updating, and abstention each win. This literature search is a reasoned assessment of overlap; it cannot certify worldwide priority.

## A publication decision the authors can act on

| Proposed submission | Decision today | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Real-QPU adaptive surface-code improvement or real-time decoder paper | **Reject current claim** | Ordered device registers and ground-truth mapping, physical execution of the claimed path, a fair replay or interleaved live comparison, and measured timing. Offline replay alone can support an offline paper. |
| New simulation algorithm outperforming existing methods | **Do not submit the current draft** | A properly time-varying problem, equally informed opponents, measured update costs, a strong regularized baseline, independent seeds, and a practically meaningful benefit. |
| Simulation methods or negative-results paper | **Most promising direction** | Reproduce the historical failure, explain the mechanism, then predict and verify the conditions where a gate or simple regularization pays off on new runs. A careful simulation-only paper is legitimate. |
| Research software or workshop artifact | **Plausible after repair** | A clean public release with one working scientific example, realistic scope, trustworthy provenance, usable entry points, and evidence a researcher can reproduce. Acceptance is venue-dependent. |
| Professor meeting or internal research milestone | **Ready now** | Show the actual comparison, the failed controls, the rehabilitated historical result, and the next discriminating experiment. |

### Experiments in order of scientific value

1. **Freeze the historical record.** Preserve the recovered generating code and original data with a dated correction. Identify the exact command, dependency versions, output normalization, and which controller each table evaluates. Correct the sleep-based table, replay provenance, observable mapping, mislabeled windows and incorrect citations before circulating a manuscript as a research result.
2. **Build a challenge with genuine temporal structure.** Start from an independently calibrated reference, generate an observable pre-change period, randomized onset, duration, magnitude, locality and recovery. Include no-change, low-drift and briefly changed controls, correlated noise and multiple hotspot locations. Inject faults with Stim's circuit API or explicit flattened operations and assert the intended targets and counts. Use both logical bases if claiming general memory performance. Keep a stable persistent-mismatch task as a separate benchmark.
3. **Make the decision causal and the baselines fair.** Score batch t with the graph chosen before t; use its syndrome only to choose the graph for t+1. In a separate buffered-batch experiment allow every method the same lookahead and publish its delay. Compare static, always-update, fixed shrinkage, tuned shrinkage/EWMA, a window-tuned literature method and the proposed gate. Include factory and matched-noise references. Tune windows and hyperparameters on a distinct pilot partition. A particularly informative control is a fixed-period updater or random schedule matched to the gate's number of graph rebuilds.
4. **Choose the scientific success condition before the new data.** For example, reduce logical errors relative to the strongest eligible baseline at a fixed graph-rebuild budget, while keeping the upper confidence bound on excess LER in stationary noise below an application-derived margin. State the trajectory, device session, or calibration block as the independent unit. Prespecify a single primary contrast or an explicit multiplicity family; publish the raw and adjusted results and all zero-difference seeds. Use the planned test for power, and measure variance in an independent pilot. Include run-level action logs, update delay, update utility, and empirical false alarms per hour or per block.
5. **Measure actual runtime.** Time decode, gate, probability fitting, graph reconstruction and dispatch separately for STAY, REWEIGHT and RESET on real arrays. Report warm-up, hardware, threads, W, d, medians and tail latencies, queueing/backlog and whether collection overlaps computation. Record machine-readable timings. Include CPU cost in a declared utility or compare at equal compute. A faster decision with a worse logical-error rate can have real value if the resource trade-off is measured.
6. **Use real data strategically.** Recover original IBM registers if the authors have access, verify per-shot order, use the same syndromes for all decoders, and validate the logical mapping against known injected faults. If the scientific claim is an effect of physical DD, interleave randomized, repeated matched circuit blocks across calibrations and retain transpiled schedules. For graph-update policy testing, a public data replay with documented chronology can be cheaper and more informative than a new two-job pilot. Extend to d = 5 and 7 in simulation with comparable round conventions. Keep hardware and simulator estimates separate.
7. **Use a stopping rule for the idea itself.** If the gate fails to improve on regularized or periodic updating under equal compute and a declared safety margin on fresh data, publish the negative or regime-dependent result if it generalizes, or retire the controller-superiority claim. If a consistent advantage survives at more than one distance and on an independent dataset or independently generated drift family, a specialist methods submission becomes defensible. More features, longer proofs, and more test counts alone will not resolve the scientific question.

**Bottom line for the authors.** The strongest thing you can already say is that you traced a real estimation-versus-abstention trade-off through surface-code circuits and discovered a regime in which your own gate misses an opportunity even a simple prior blend can use.
